# Exercise Form and Safety Guide

## Essential Exercise Form Cues

### Squats
**Setup:**
- Feet shoulder-width apart, toes slightly out
- Bar on upper traps (high bar) or rear delts (low bar)
- Chest up, core braced

**Movement:**
- Break at hips and knees simultaneously
- Keep knees tracking over toes
- Descend until thighs are parallel or below
- Drive through heels to return to start
- Keep core tight throughout

**Common Mistakes:**
- Knees caving inward
- Rounding lower back
- Heels lifting off ground
- Not reaching adequate depth

**Safety:**
- Use safety bars/spotter when going heavy
- Start with bodyweight to learn form
- Don't bounce at the bottom

---

### Deadlifts
**Setup:**
- Bar over mid-foot
- Feet hip-width apart
- Grip outside legs
- Shoulders slightly in front of bar
- Neutral spine, chest up

**Movement:**
- Push through floor with legs
- Keep bar close to body
- Hips and shoulders rise together
- Extend hips and knees to full lockout
- Reverse motion to lower

**Common Mistakes:**
- Rounding lower back
- Bar drifting away from body
- Starting with hips too low or too high
- Hyperextending at the top

**Safety:**
- Keep core braced throughout
- Don't jerk the weight off floor
- Use mixed or hook grip for heavy weights
- Consider lifting straps if grip is limiting

---

### Bench Press
**Setup:**
- Lie on bench with eyes under bar
- Feet flat on floor
- Arch lower back slightly
- Shoulder blades squeezed together
- Grip slightly wider than shoulder-width

**Movement:**
- Unrack with straight arms
- Lower bar to mid-chest in controlled manner
- Keep elbows at ~45-degree angle
- Press bar up and slightly back
- Maintain tension in upper back

**Common Mistakes:**
- Bouncing bar off chest
- Lifting butt off bench
- Flaring elbows too wide
- Losing upper back tightness

**Safety:**
- Always use a spotter for heavy sets
- Use safety bars if training alone
- Don't lift beyond your capabilities

---

### Overhead Press
**Setup:**
- Bar at shoulder height
- Grip slightly wider than shoulders
- Elbows slightly forward
- Core braced, glutes tight

**Movement:**
- Press bar straight up
- Move head back slightly as bar passes face
- Lock out arms overhead
- Bar finishes over mid-foot
- Lower under control

**Common Mistakes:**
- Pressing bar forward (not straight up)
- Excessive back arch
- Not fully locking out
- Losing core tension

**Safety:**
- Start light to learn proper path
- Don't lean back excessively
- Use rack if unable to complete rep

---

### Pull-ups/Lat Pulldowns
**Setup:**
- Grip bar slightly wider than shoulders
- Hang with arms fully extended
- Chest up, shoulders back

**Movement:**
- Pull elbows down and back
- Lead with chest, not chin
- Pull until chin over bar (pull-ups)
- Squeeze shoulder blades together at top
- Lower under control

**Common Mistakes:**
- Using momentum (kipping)
- Partial range of motion
- Shrugging shoulders
- Not engaging lats

**Safety:**
- Build up to full pull-ups with negatives or assistance
- Avoid swinging

---

### Rows (Barbell, Dumbbell)
**Setup:**
- Hinge at hips, back flat
- Slight knee bend
- Arms hanging straight down
- Neutral spine

**Movement:**
- Pull weight to torso
- Elbows close to body
- Squeeze shoulder blades at top
- Control the descent
- Maintain flat back throughout

**Common Mistakes:**
- Rounding back
- Using too much momentum
- Not pulling to full contraction
- Standing too upright

**Safety:**
- Don't sacrifice back position for heavier weight
- Keep core braced

## General Safety Principles

### Warm-up Protocol
1. **General warm-up** (5 min): Light cardio to increase body temperature
2. **Dynamic stretching** (5 min): Movement-based stretches for joints and muscles
3. **Specific warm-up**: Light sets of working exercises with increasing weight

### Progressive Overload
- Increase weight by 2.5-5 lbs for upper body, 5-10 lbs for lower body
- Add reps when you can complete all sets with good form
- Increase sets before increasing weight significantly
- Deload (reduce volume/intensity) every 4-6 weeks

### Rest Periods
- **Strength focus** (heavy weight): 3-5 minutes
- **Hypertrophy focus** (moderate weight): 60-90 seconds
- **Endurance focus** (light weight): 30-60 seconds
- **Between exercises**: As needed to maintain quality

### Breathing
- **Exhale during exertion** (lifting/pushing/pulling phase)
- **Inhale during easier phase** (lowering phase)
- **Brace core**: Hold breath during heavy compound lifts (Valsalva maneuver)
- Never hold breath for entire exercise

### Injury Prevention
- Master form with light weight before adding load
- Don't train through sharp pain
- Respect rest days
- Address muscle imbalances
- Include mobility work
- Get adequate sleep and nutrition
- Listen to your body

### When to Stop and Seek Medical Advice
- Sharp, acute pain (especially in joints)
- Pain that persists days after workout
- Numbness or tingling
- Severe muscle strains
- Chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Dizziness or nausea

## Exercise Modifications

### For Beginners
- **Squats**: Box squats, goblet squats
- **Deadlifts**: Romanian deadlifts, kettlebell deadlifts
- **Bench Press**: Dumbbell press, push-ups
- **Pull-ups**: Lat pulldowns, assisted pull-ups, negative pull-ups
- **Overhead Press**: Seated press, dumbbell press

### For Limited Mobility
- **Hip mobility**: Elevate heels for squats
- **Shoulder mobility**: Use dumbbells instead of barbell
- **Back issues**: Focus on machines, avoid loaded spinal flexion
- **Knee issues**: Reduce range of motion, use leg press

### For Home Workouts (Minimal Equipment)
- **No barbell**: Use dumbbells or resistance bands
- **No bench**: Use floor, stability ball, or incline surface
- **No pull-up bar**: Use resistance bands, TRX, or inverted rows
- **No equipment**: Bodyweight progressions (pistol squats, one-arm push-ups)

## Recovery and Regeneration

### Active Recovery
- Light cardio (walking, cycling, swimming)
- Yoga or stretching
- Foam rolling
- Light calisthenics

### Sleep
- Aim for 7-9 hours per night
- Maintain consistent sleep schedule
- Create optimal sleep environment
- Most muscle recovery happens during sleep

### Nutrition for Recovery
- Adequate protein (muscle repair)
- Carbohydrates (glycogen replenishment)
- Hydration (transport nutrients, remove waste)
- Anti-inflammatory foods (berries, fatty fish, leafy greens)

### Managing Soreness
- Light movement and stretching
- Foam rolling or massage
- Ice or heat (as preferred)
- Adequate hydration
- Proper nutrition
- Time (DOMS typically resolves in 48-72 hours)

---

Remember: **Form > Weight > Volume**

Always prioritize proper technique over lifting heavier weights or doing more reps. Quality movement builds long-term strength and prevents injuries.
